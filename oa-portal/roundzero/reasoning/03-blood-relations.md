# Blood relations

Short topic, guaranteed 1–3 questions, and almost impossible to get wrong once you draw instead of reason in your head.

## The rule: always draw

Never try to hold a family tree in your head. Use one consistent notation:

```
        +  = male            -  = female
        =  = married         |  = parent/child (vertical)
        —  = sibling (horizontal)
```

A worked layout:

```
        A(+) = B(-)
              |
      ------------------
      |                |
    C(+)             D(-)
```

Generations run **downwards**. Siblings sit on the same horizontal line. That is the whole system.

## The relationship vocabulary

| Phrase | Means |
|---|---|
| Father's / mother's son | brother (or self) |
| Father's / mother's daughter | sister (or self) |
| Father's brother | paternal uncle |
| Father's sister | paternal aunt |
| Mother's brother | maternal uncle |
| Mother's sister | maternal aunt |
| Brother's / sister's son | nephew |
| Brother's / sister's daughter | niece |
| Son's / daughter's son | grandson |
| Husband's / wife's father | father-in-law |
| Husband's / wife's brother | brother-in-law |
| Sister's husband | brother-in-law |
| Only son of one's grandfather | one's father (or self) |

> **"Only son of my father" is *me* if I am male.** This is the trick the whole topic is
> built around — see the pointing question below.

## Type 1 — descriptive

*Pointing to a man, a woman said, "His mother is the only daughter of my mother." How is the woman related to the man?*

**Work from the inside out:**
- "the only daughter of my mother" — the speaker's mother has exactly one daughter, and the
  speaker is a woman, so that daughter is **the speaker herself**.
- So "his mother is the speaker" → the woman is the man's **mother**.

*Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son."*

- "my father's son" — he has no brothers, so that is **himself**.
- So "that man's father is me" → the photograph is of his **son**.

**The method:** replace every clause with a person, starting from the innermost, and rewrite the sentence as you go. Never try to parse it in one pass.

## Type 2 — coded relations

Symbols stand for relationships:

- **A + B** means A is the father of B
- **A − B** means A is the wife of B
- **A × B** means A is the brother of B
- **A ÷ B** means A is the mother of B

*What does P + Q − R mean?*

- P + Q → P is the father of Q
- Q − R → Q is the wife of R
- So P is the **father of R's wife** → P is R's **father-in-law**

**Method:** decode left to right, draw as you go, then read the required relation off the diagram.

*Which of the following means "M is the grandmother of N"?*
- You need M ÷ (someone) and (that someone) is a parent of N.
- M ÷ X, X + N → **M ÷ X + N** ✓

## Type 3 — puzzle form

*A is B's brother. C is A's father. D is C's sister. E is D's mother. How is B related to E?*

Draw it:

```
        E(-)
         |
   ------------
   |          |
 C(+)       D(-)
   |
 -------
 |     |
A(+)  B(?)
```

- E is C's mother (since E is D's mother and C, D are siblings)
- C is A and B's father
- So E is A and B's **grandmother**, meaning **B is E's grandchild**.

Since B's gender is not stated, "grandson" would be an assumption — the answer is  
**grandchild** (or "grandson/granddaughter" if the options force it).

## Practice set

#### Q1. Pointing to a photograph, a woman says, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is she related to the woman?

Grandfather's only son = the woman's father. His daughter = the woman herself or her sister.  
Since she is pointing at a photograph of someone else → **her sister**

#### Q2. A man said, "This woman's mother-in-law is my mother." How is the woman related to him?

His mother is her mother-in-law → she married his mother's son → she is his **wife** (or his brother's wife). With no brother mentioned, **wife**.

#### Q3. Introducing a man, a woman said, "His wife is the only daughter of my mother." How is the man related to her?

The only daughter of her mother is the woman herself → the man's wife is her → **husband**

#### Q4. Pointing to a man, a boy said, "He is the son of my grandfather's only child." How is the man related to the boy?

Grandfather's only child = the boy's father. His son = the boy or his brother.  
Since the boy points at someone else → **his brother**

#### Q5. A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. E is D's mother. How is A related to D?

C is B's mother, and A is B's sister, so C is also A's mother. D is C's father → D is A's  
**grandfather** → A is D's **granddaughter**.

#### Q6. If P + Q means P is the mother of Q, P − Q means P is the brother of Q, and P × Q means P is the father of Q, what does M × N + O mean?

M × N → M is N's father. N + O → N is O's mother.  
So M is the father of O's mother → M is O's **maternal grandfather**.

#### Q7. Using the same symbols, which expression means "A is the grandmother of C"?

A must be a mother, and her child must be a parent of C.  
A + B (A is B's mother), then B + C or B × C → **A + B + C** or **A + B × C**

#### Q8. Pointing to a lady, Rahul said, "The son of her only brother is the brother of my wife." How is the lady related to Rahul's wife?

The lady's brother's son is Rahul's wife's brother. So the lady's brother is the father of  
Rahul's wife → the lady is Rahul's wife's **father's sister = paternal aunt**.

#### Q9. X is the husband of Y. Z is the only daughter of X. W is the mother of Y. How is W related to Z?

Y is Z's mother; W is Y's mother → W is Z's **maternal grandmother**.

#### Q10. A and B are sisters. C is the mother of A. D is the father of C. What is B to D?

C is mother of A, and A and B are sisters, so C is B's mother too. D is C's father → D is B's grandfather → **B is D's granddaughter**.

#### Q11. Pointing to a photo, Meera said, "He is the only son of my mother's only son." How is the boy related to Meera?

Her mother's only son = Meera's brother. His only son = her brother's son → **her nephew**

#### Q12. If A is the brother of B, B is the sister of C, and C is the father of D, how is A related to D?

C is D's father, and A is C's brother (via B being sister to both) → A is D's **paternal uncle**.

> Notice the pattern: **every single one of these becomes trivial once drawn.** The difficulty
> is entirely in parsing the sentence, never in the family logic.

## Traps

- **Gender is not always given.** "A is the child of B" does not tell you A's sex. Do not
  pick "nephew" when "niece" is equally possible — look for an option that avoids the assumption.
- **"Only son/daughter of X"** almost always resolves to the speaker themselves. Check the
  speaker's stated gender.
- **In-law vs blood.** Sister's husband and wife's brother are both "brother-in-law", but a
  brother-in-law is not a brother.
- **Cousin** has no gender in English. If a question wants gender, the answer is not
  "cousin".
- **Maternal vs paternal.** Mother's brother is maternal uncle; father's brother is paternal
  uncle. Questions specify this to distinguish otherwise-identical options.
- Read the direction: "How is A related to B" is not the same as "How is B related to A".
