# Percentages

Learn this one properly and profit & loss, interest, and half of data interpretation come free — they are all percentages wearing different clothes.

## What gets asked

1. Find x% of y (fast)
2. Percentage change, and **successive** changes
3. "A is x% more than B — B is what % less than A?"
4. Population / price / salary chains
5. Percentage as the backbone of a DI question

---

## The fraction table — memorise this

This is the highest-value 10 minutes in the whole shelf. It turns multiplication into division.

| Fraction | % | Fraction | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50 | 1/9 | 11.11 |
| 1/3 | 33.33 | 1/10 | 10 |
| 1/4 | 25 | 1/11 | 9.09 |
| 1/5 | 20 | 1/12 | 8.33 |
| 1/6 | 16.66 | 1/16 | 6.25 |
| 1/7 | 14.28 | 1/20 | 5 |
| 1/8 | 12.5 | 1/25 | 4 |

Also worth knowing: 3/4 = 75, 2/3 = 66.66, 3/8 = 37.5, 5/8 = 62.5, 2/5 = 40, 3/5 = 60.

### Worked example — 37.5% of 512

- 37.5% = **3/8**
- 512 × 3/8 = 64 × 3 = **192**

No multiplication of decimals. That is the whole point of the table.

## The two core moves

**Percentage change** = (new − old) / **old** × 100. The denominator is always the *old* value — this is the single most common careless error in the section.

**x% of y = y% of x.** So 16% of 25 is the same as 25% of 16 = 4. Use whichever is easier.

---

## Successive changes

Two changes of a% and b% combine to:

> **net % = a + b + (ab / 100)**

Use signs: a decrease is negative.

### Worked example — price rises 20%, then falls 20%

- net = 20 + (−20) + (20 × −20)/100 = 0 − 4 = **−4%**, i.e. a 4% *decrease*.

You do not get back to where you started. This is asked constantly.

### Worked example — 10,000 population, +10% then −10%

10,000 × 1.1 × 0.9 = **9,900**. Same result, and matches the −4%-style logic.

## The reversal formula

If **A is x% more than B**, then **B is (x / (100 + x)) × 100 % less than A**.  
If **A is x% less than B**, then **B is (x / (100 − x)) × 100 % more than A**.

### Worked example — salary rises 25%. What cut returns it to the original?

- x = 25 → 25 / 125 × 100 = **20%**

**Shortcut:** convert to a fraction. +25% is ×5/4, so reversing is ×4/5, a drop of 1/5 =  
**20%**. The fraction table makes this instant:

| Change | As a fraction | Reverse |
|---|---|---|
| +25% | × 5/4 | −20% |
| +20% | × 6/5 | −16.66% |
| +50% | × 3/2 | −33.33% |
| +100% | × 2 | −50% |
| −20% | × 4/5 | +25% |

---

## Working backwards

If a value **after** an x% increase is V, the original is **V / (1 + x/100)** — divide, do not subtract x%.

### Worked example — a number increased by 20% becomes 480. Find it.

- 480 / 1.2 = **400**. (Not 480 − 96 = 384. That is the trap.)

## Worked example — a full exam-style question

*In an election between two candidates, the winner got 56% of the votes and won by 1,440 votes. How many votes were cast?*

- Winner 56% → loser 44% → margin = 12% of the total.
- 12% = 1,440 → 1% = 120 → **total = 12,000 votes**.

**The 15-second way:** margin percentage = 56 − 44 = 12. Total = 1440 × 100/12 = 12,000.

---

## Worked example — consumption and price

*The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what percentage must a family cut consumption to keep its expenditure unchanged?*

- Expenditure = price × quantity, and it must stay fixed.
- Price × 5/4 means quantity must be × 4/5 → a drop of 1/5 = **20%**.

This is the reversal formula again. Recognising it saves the derivation.

## Practice set

#### Q1. What is 62.5% of 848?

62.5% = 5/8 → 848 × 5/8 = 106 × 5 = **530**

#### Q2. If 15% of a number is 45, what is 40% of the same number?

15% = 45 → 1% = 3 → 40% = **120**  
*(Faster: 40/15 = 8/3, so 45 × 8/3 = 120.)*

#### Q3. A number is increased by 25% and then decreased by 25%. Find the net change.

Net = 25 − 25 − (25 × 25)/100 = −6.25 → **6.25% decrease**

#### Q4. The price of an article is reduced by 20%. By what percentage must it be increased to restore the original price?

−20% is × 4/5, so reversing needs × 5/4 = +1/4.

**Answer: 25%**

#### Q5. In an examination, 35% of students failed in Maths, 45% failed in English, and 20% failed in both. What percentage passed in both?

Failed in at least one = 35 + 45 − 20 = 60%.

**Answer: 40% passed in both**

#### Q6. A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what percentage is B's salary less than A's?

25/(100+25) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = **20%**

#### Q7. The population of a town increases 10% in the first year and decreases 10% in the second. If it is now 29,700, what was it originally?

P × 1.1 × 0.9 = 29,700 → P × 0.99 = 29,700 → P = **30,000**

#### Q8. If the price of petrol rises by 20%, by what percentage must consumption be reduced so that expenditure rises by only 8%?

New expenditure factor = 1.08, price factor = 1.2 → consumption factor = 1.08/1.2 = 0.9.

**Answer: 10% reduction**

#### Q9. A candidate scoring 30% fails by 60 marks. Another scoring 45% gets 30 marks more than the pass mark. Find the total marks.

The difference between the two scores is 15% of the total.  
That difference in marks = 60 + 30 = 90.  
15% = 90 → total = **600 marks** *(pass mark = 30% of 600 + 60 = 180 + 60 = 240)*

#### Q10. Two numbers are 20% and 50% more than a third number. What percentage is the first of the second?

Let the third be 100 → first = 120, second = 150.  
120/150 × 100 = **80%**

#### Q11. The length of a rectangle increases by 20% and the breadth decreases by 20%. Find the percentage change in area.

Net = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = **−4%**, i.e. the area falls by 4%.

#### Q12. In an election with two candidates, 20% of the votes were invalid. The winner got 60% of the valid votes and won by 1,200 votes. Find the total votes cast.

Margin among valid votes = 60 − 40 = 20% of the valid votes.  
20% of valid = 1,200 → valid votes = 6,000.  
Valid votes are 80% of the total → total = 6,000 / 0.8 = **7,500**  
*Check: valid 6,000 → winner 3,600, loser 2,400, margin 1,200 ✓*

> Note which base the margin sits on. "Won by 20% of the **valid** votes" and "20% of the
> **total** votes" give different answers, and the question always specifies one.

## Traps

- Percentage change divides by the **old** value, never the new one.
- Successive changes never simply add. +20% then +30% is +56%, not +50%.
- "x% more than" and "x% of" are different: 20% more than 50 is 60; 20% of 50 is 10.
- Percentage **points** ≠ percent. Going from 40% to 44% is a rise of 4 percentage points
  but a **10%** increase.
- When two different bases are involved (marks of A vs marks of B), never add the
  percentages directly.

- [ ] I know that +20% then −20% is a net 4% loss, and why
- [ ] I can reverse a percentage change using the x/(100±x) formula or the fraction trick
