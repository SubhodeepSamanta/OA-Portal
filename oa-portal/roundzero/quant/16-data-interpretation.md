# Data interpretation

DI is not a maths topic — it is a **reading speed and approximation** topic. The arithmetic is trivial; the marks are lost to panic and to over-precision.

## What gets asked

1. Table
2. Bar chart / line graph
3. Pie chart (often two pies to be compared)
4. Caselet (a paragraph of data, no picture)
5. Mixed / missing-data sets

A DI set is normally 3–5 questions on one dataset. **The first 30 seconds you spend reading the set properly pays for all of them.**

---

## The method

**Step 1 — read the headings, not the numbers.** What is the unit? Thousands? Crores?  
Percentages or absolutes? Is one row a total?

**Step 2 — find the question type.** Almost every DI question is one of five:

| Type | What to do |
|---|---|
| Direct lookup | just read the cell |
| Total / average | add the row or column |
| Percentage / share | part ÷ whole × 100 |
| Ratio between two entries | divide; do not compute both values |
| Percentage **change** between years | (new − old)/old |

**Step 3 — approximate.** Look at how far apart the options are before calculating. If they are 12%, 18%, 24%, 31%, you need one significant figure, not three.

---

## The approximation rules that win the section

- **Round to 2 significant figures.** 4,978 → 5,000. 23.4 → 23.
- **Use the fraction table** from the percentages sheet. "What percent is 1,470 of 5,880?"
  → 1470/5880 = 1/4 = **25%**.
- **Compare fractions by cross-multiplication**, never by dividing both.
  Is 47/128 bigger than 35/97? 47 × 97 = 4559 vs 35 × 128 = 4480 → yes, the first.
- **Percentage change ≈ difference ÷ original.** For a rise from 240 to 300: 60/240 = **25%**.
- Never compute anything the question did not ask for.

### Worked example — a ratio question

*Sales (₹ lakh): 2021 = 4,850; 2022 = 5,820. Ratio of 2021 to 2022?*

- Do **not** divide. Both are divisible by 970: 4850/970 = 5, 5820/970 = 6 → **5 : 6**
- Even without spotting that: 4850/5820 ≈ 0.833 = 5/6.

### Worked example — percentage change

*Revenue rose from ₹1,250 cr to ₹1,500 cr. Percentage increase?*

- 250/1250 = 1/5 = **20%**

## Pie charts

A pie chart gives **shares**, not amounts. To get an amount you need the total, which is always given somewhere in the question stem.

- **Degrees to percent:** divide by 3.6. A 54° sector is 54/3.6 = **15%**.
- **Percent to degrees:** multiply by 3.6.

### Worked example

*A company's ₹7,200 cr expenditure is split as a pie. The R&D sector is 45°. What does R&D cost?*

- 45/360 = 1/8 → 7200/8 = **₹900 cr**

Use the fraction, not the percentage — 1/8 is faster than 12.5%.

### Two-pie comparison

The commonest DI trap: two pie charts with **different totals**. A slice that is 20% of a  
₹500 cr pie is smaller than a slice that is 15% of a ₹900 cr pie. Always convert to absolute values before comparing across the two charts.

## Tables and bar charts

### Worked example — a small table

| Year | Product A | Product B | Product C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 320 | 280 | 400 |
| 2021 | 400 | 350 | 450 |
| 2022 | 480 | 420 | 500 |

*Q1. Total sales in 2021?* → 400 + 350 + 450 = **1,200**

*Q2. Which product grew fastest from 2020 to 2022?*
- A: 320 → 480, growth 160/320 = **50%**
- B: 280 → 420, growth 140/280 = **50%**
- C: 400 → 500, growth 100/400 = 25%
- **A and B tie at 50%.** Note the question asks about *growth rate*, not absolute growth —
  by absolute growth A wins with 160.

*Q3. A's share of total 2022 sales?*
- Total 2022 = 480 + 420 + 500 = 1,400
- 480/1400 = 24/70 = 12/35 ≈ **34.3%**

That third question is where over-precision costs time: if the options are 28%, 34%, 41%,  
46%, then "480 out of 1400 is a bit over a third" settles it in three seconds.

## Caselets

A caselet hides a table inside a paragraph. **Draw the table first.** Do not attempt any question until the data is in a grid — you will misread it otherwise.

## Practice set

### Set A — table

Sales of four products (in ₹ thousand):

| Year | P | Q | R | S | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 250 | 180 | 320 | 150 | 900 |
| 2021 | 300 | 240 | 360 | 200 | 1100 |
| 2022 | 375 | 300 | 400 | 225 | 1300 |
| 2023 | 450 | 330 | 440 | 280 | 1500 |

#### Q1. What is the percentage increase in P's sales from 2020 to 2023?

(450 − 250)/250 = 200/250 = 4/5 = **80%**

#### Q2. Which product showed the highest percentage growth over 2020–2023?

- P: 200/250 = 80% · Q: 150/180 = 83.3% · R: 120/320 = 37.5% · S: 130/150 = 86.7%
**S**, at 86.7%. *(Note P has the largest absolute growth but not the largest percentage — read which is asked.)*

#### Q3. In 2022, what percentage of total sales came from R?

400/1300 = 4/13 ≈ **30.8%**

#### Q4. What is the average annual sales of Q over the four years?

(180 + 240 + 300 + 330)/4 = 1050/4 = **262.5 thousand**

#### Q5. In which year did total sales grow the fastest?

- 2021: 200/900 = 22.2% · 2022: 200/1100 = 18.2% · 2023: 200/1300 = 15.4%
**2021.** *(The absolute rise is 200 every year — only the base changes, so the growth rate must fall. Spotting that answers it without any division.)*

#### Q6. The ratio of P to R in 2023?

450 : 440 = **45 : 44**

### Set B — pie chart

A family's monthly budget of ₹36,000 is divided as: Food 30%, Rent 25%, Education 15%,  
Transport 10%, Savings 12%, Others 8%.

#### Q7. How much is spent on Rent?

25% of 36,000 = **₹9,000**

#### Q8. What is the central angle for Education?

15 × 3.6 = **54°**

#### Q9. How much more is spent on Food than on Transport?

(30 − 10)% = 20% of 36,000 = **₹7,200**

#### Q10. Savings and Others together form what angle?

(12 + 8) = 20% → 20 × 3.6 = **72°**

#### Q11. If the family wants to save 20% instead, keeping Rent and Education fixed, by what amount must the remaining categories be cut?

Extra saving needed = (20 − 12)% = 8% of 36,000 = **₹2,880** to be cut from Food, Transport and Others combined.

### Set C — the two-pie trap

Company X's revenue is ₹500 crore, split: Domestic 60%, Export 40%.  
Company Y's revenue is ₹900 crore, split: Domestic 45%, Export 55%.

#### Q12. Whose domestic revenue is higher?

- X: 60% of 500 = ₹300 cr
- Y: 45% of 900 = ₹405 cr
**Y**, despite having the smaller domestic *percentage*.

> This is the single most common DI trap in the whole section. **Never compare percentages
> across two charts with different totals — convert to absolutes first.**

#### Q13. What is the ratio of X's export revenue to Y's export revenue?

X: 40% of 500 = 200 · Y: 55% of 900 = 495  
200 : 495 = **40 : 99**

### Set D — caselet

*In a class of 120 students, 70 play cricket, 55 play football, and 25 play both.*

#### Q14. How many play at least one sport?

70 + 55 − 25 = **100**

#### Q15. How many play neither?

120 − 100 = **20**

#### Q16. How many play only cricket?

70 − 25 = **45**

## Traps

- **Percentage of what?** "B is what percent of A" and "A is what percent more than B" are
  different questions with different denominators.
- **Units.** A chart in thousands and a question in lakhs. Check once, at the start.
- **"Approximately"** in the question stem is permission to round hard. Take it.
- Comparing across two pie charts with different totals.
- A question asking for "the increase" wants an absolute number; "percentage increase" wants
  a ratio. Read the last three words of the question.
- Do not answer from memory of an earlier sub-question — the year or the product often
  changes between them.

---

## Checkpoints

- [ ] I read the headings, units and totals before looking at any number
- [ ] I check how far apart the options are before deciding how precisely to calculate
- [ ] I round to two significant figures by default
- [ ] I use the fraction table instead of long division for percentage shares
- [ ] I compare fractions by cross-multiplying rather than dividing both
- [ ] I convert pie-chart degrees to shares by dividing by 3.6
- [ ] I convert to absolute values before comparing across two pie charts with different totals
- [ ] I draw the table out before attempting a caselet
- [ ] I re-read the last few words of each question to catch "increase" vs "percentage increase"
