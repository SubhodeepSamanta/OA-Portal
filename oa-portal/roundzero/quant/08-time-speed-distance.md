# Time, speed & distance

Named by every source as a top-3 highest-weightage topic. Structurally identical to time & work: rate × time = quantity.

## What gets asked

1. Straight distance/speed/time substitution
2. Average speed over a round trip
3. "If he had walked x km/h faster he would have arrived n minutes earlier"
4. Two bodies meeting / overtaking
5. Relative speed (also the whole trains sheet)

---

## The base

> **Distance = Speed × Time**

**The conversion you will use in every second question:**

> **km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18**
> **m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5**

72 km/h = 20 m/s. 36 km/h = 10 m/s. Know these two by sight.

---

## Inverse proportionality — the real shortcut

For a **fixed distance**, speed and time are inversely proportional:

> **speed ratio a : b → time ratio b : a**

This one line solves most of the "he would have arrived earlier" family without algebra.

### Worked example

*A boy walking at 4/5 of his usual speed reaches school 10 minutes late. What is his usual time?*

- Speed ratio new : usual = 4 : 5 → time ratio = 5 : 4
- The extra 1 part = 10 minutes → usual time = 4 parts = **40 minutes**

### Worked example

*Walking at 5 km/h a man reaches office 6 minutes late; at 6 km/h he is 2 minutes early.  
Find the distance.*

- Speed ratio 5 : 6 → time ratio 6 : 5, so the difference is 1 part.
- Time difference between the two journeys = 6 + 2 = 8 minutes → 1 part = 8 min.
- Time at 5 km/h = 6 parts = 48 min = 0.8 h → distance = 5 × 0.8 = **4 km**

## Average speed

- **Equal distances** at a and b: **2ab / (a + b)** (harmonic mean)
- **Equal times** at a and b: **(a + b) / 2**
- Otherwise: **total distance / total time**, always.

### Worked example

*A car covers the first 100 km at 50 km/h and the next 100 km at 25 km/h.*

- 2 × 50 × 25 / 75 = 2500/75 = **33.33 km/h**

Never average the speeds when the distances are equal.

## Relative speed

| Direction | Relative speed |
|---|---|
| Opposite (approaching or crossing) | **a + b** |
| Same direction (overtaking) | **a − b** |

**Time to meet = gap / relative speed.**

### Worked example — approaching

*Two towns are 330 km apart. A car leaves A at 60 km/h and another leaves B at 50 km/h at the same time, towards each other. When do they meet?*

- Relative speed = 110 km/h → 330 / 110 = **3 hours**

### Worked example — overtaking with a head start

*A thief runs at 8 km/h. A policeman starts chasing 30 minutes later at 10 km/h. When does he catch him?*

- Head start = 8 × 0.5 = 4 km
- Relative speed = 10 − 8 = 2 km/h
- Time = 4 / 2 = **2 hours** after the policeman starts.

## Circular tracks

Two people on a circular track of length L at speeds a and b:

| | Opposite directions | Same direction |
|---|---|---|
| Time to meet **anywhere** | L / (a + b) | L / (a − b) |
| Time to meet at the **start** | LCM of L/a and L/b | LCM of L/a and L/b |

### Worked example

*A 400 m track. A runs at 8 m/s, B at 6 m/s, in opposite directions. First meeting?*

- 400 / (8 + 6) = 400/14 = **28.57 s**

## The "would have" family

These all reduce to one equation. Let the distance be d.

### Worked example

*A man travels a certain distance at 6 km/h and returns at 4 km/h, taking 5 hours in total.  
Find the one-way distance.*

- d/6 + d/4 = 5
- Multiply by 12: 2d + 3d = 60 → 5d = 60 → **d = 12 km**

### Worked example

*If a train travelled 10 km/h faster it would take 1 hour less for a 300 km journey. Find its speed.*

- 300/s − 300/(s+10) = 1
- 300(s + 10) − 300s = s(s + 10) → 3000 = s² + 10s
- s² + 10s − 3000 = 0 → (s + 60)(s − 50) = 0 → **s = 50 km/h**
- Check: 300/50 = 6 h, 300/60 = 5 h. Difference exactly 1. ✓

**Faster in an exam:** do not solve the quadratic — test the options. Each one takes two divisions and a subtraction, and the right answer announces itself. With four options you will normally hit it in two tries.

## Practice set

#### Q1. Convert 90 km/h to m/s, and 25 m/s to km/h.

90 × 5/18 = **25 m/s** · 25 × 18/5 = **90 km/h**

#### Q2. A car covers 240 km in 4 hours. What distance will it cover in 7 hours at the same speed?

Speed = 60 km/h → 60 × 7 = **420 km**

#### Q3. Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man is 20 minutes late. Find his usual time.

Speed ratio 3 : 4 → time ratio 4 : 3. The extra 1 part = 20 min → usual time = 3 parts =  
**60 minutes**

#### Q4. A man walking at 4 km/h reaches office 5 minutes late; at 5 km/h he is 4 minutes early. Find the distance.

Speed ratio 4 : 5 → time ratio 5 : 4, difference 1 part.  
Time difference = 5 + 4 = 9 min → 1 part = 9 min.  
Time at 4 km/h = 5 parts = 45 min = 0.75 h → distance = 4 × 0.75 = **3 km**

#### Q5. A train covers a distance at 60 km/h and returns at 90 km/h. Find the average speed.

2 × 60 × 90 / 150 = 10800/150 = **72 km/h**

#### Q6. Two cities are 400 km apart. Car A leaves X at 50 km/h and car B leaves Y at 30 km/h simultaneously, towards each other. When and where do they meet?

Relative speed = 80 km/h → time = 400/80 = **5 hours**  
A has travelled 50 × 5 = **250 km from X**

#### Q7. A thief is spotted 200 m ahead by a policeman. The thief runs at 10 km/h and the policeman chases at 12 km/h. How far does the thief run before being caught?

Relative speed = 2 km/h. Gap 0.2 km → time = 0.1 h.  
Thief runs 10 × 0.1 = 1 km = **1,000 m**

#### Q8. A man covers a distance of 600 km partly by train at 100 km/h and partly by car at 60 km/h. The whole journey takes 8 hours. Find the distance by train.

Let train distance = x → x/100 + (600 − x)/60 = 8  
Multiply by 300: 3x + 5(600 − x) = 2400 → 3x + 3000 − 5x = 2400 → −2x = −600 → **x = 300 km**

#### Q9. Running at 5/6 of his usual speed a man reaches his office 10 minutes late. Find his usual time.

Time ratio = 6 : 5 → extra 1 part = 10 min → usual = 5 parts = **50 minutes**

#### Q10. Two runners on a 600 m circular track run at 6 m/s and 4 m/s. When do they first meet if they run (a) in opposite directions, (b) in the same direction?

(a) 600/(6+4) = **60 seconds** (b) 600/(6−4) = **300 seconds**

#### Q11. A car travelling at 5/7 of its usual speed covers a journey in 42 minutes more than usual. Find the usual time.

Time ratio = 7 : 5 → extra 2 parts = 42 min → 1 part = 21 → usual = 5 parts = **105 minutes**

#### Q12. If a person travels at 40 km/h he reaches 11 minutes late; at 50 km/h he is 5 minutes early. Find the distance.

Speed ratio 40 : 50 = 4 : 5 → time ratio 5 : 4, difference 1 part.  
Total time difference = 11 + 5 = 16 min → 1 part = 16 min.  
Time at 40 km/h = 5 parts = 80 min = 4/3 h → distance = 40 × 4/3 = **53.33 km**

> Q3, Q9, Q11 and Q12 are all the same question. Once you see that "fraction of usual speed"
> means "invert the fraction for time", the family costs 15 seconds each.

## Traps

- Mixing units. If speed is in km/h, time must be in hours. 30 minutes is 0.5, not 30.
- Average speed ≠ average of speeds for equal distances.
- "10 minutes late" and "5 minutes early" give a **combined** difference of 15 minutes.
- In overtaking, the relative speed is the difference — using the sum is the classic slip.
- On a circular track, "meet at the starting point" is an LCM question, not a division one.
- When a body returns by the same route, the round-trip distance is 2d — but the *one-way*
  distance is usually what is asked.
