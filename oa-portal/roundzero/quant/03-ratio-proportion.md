# Ratio, proportion, partnership

Short sheet, high frequency. Most ratio questions are one line of work once you set them up with a common multiplier.

## What gets asked

1. Divide an amount in a given ratio
2. Chain two ratios into three terms (A:B and B:C → A:B:C)
3. "The ratio becomes … after adding/removing x" → one equation
4. Partnership profit sharing
5. Ages (nearly always a ratio question in disguise)

---

## The setup that solves everything

Never work with fractions. Turn a ratio into **variables with a common multiplier**:

> A : B = 3 : 5 → write A = 3k, B = 5k

Every ratio question becomes a one-variable equation the moment you do this.

---

## Dividing an amount

Split in ratio a : b : c → each share is (its part / total parts) × amount.

### Worked example — divide ₹1,350 among A, B, C in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4

- Total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9
- One part = 1350 / 9 = 150
- **A = 300, B = 450, C = 600**

Always sanity-check that the shares add back to the total.

---

## Chaining ratios

A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5. B appears as 3 and as 4 — make them match by cross-multiplying:

- Multiply the first by 4 → A : B = 8 : 12
- Multiply the second by 3 → B : C = 12 : 15
- **A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15**

**Shortcut:** A : B : C = (2×4) : (3×4) : (3×5) = 8 : 12 : 15.

## The "ratio changes" pattern

### Worked example

*The ratio of two numbers is 3 : 4. If 8 is added to each, the ratio becomes 4 : 5. Find the numbers.*

- Let them be 3k and 4k.
- (3k + 8) / (4k + 8) = 4/5
- 5(3k + 8) = 4(4k + 8) → 15k + 40 = 16k + 32 → **k = 8**
- Numbers are **24 and 32**. Check: 32/40 = 4/5. ✓

Every question of this family is those four lines. Learn the shape, not the numbers.

## Proportion

**a : b = c : d** means **ad = bc** (product of extremes = product of means).

- **Mean proportional** between a and b = √(ab)
- **Third proportional** to a, b = b² / a
- **Fourth proportional** to a, b, c = bc / a

### Worked example — mean proportional between 4 and 25

√(4 × 25) = √100 = **10**

---

## Direct and inverse variation

- **Direct:** more men → more work. a/b = c/d
- **Inverse:** more men → less time. a × b = c × d

The chain rule for mixed problems:

> **(M₁ × D₁ × H₁) / W₁ = (M₂ × D₂ × H₂) / W₂**

(M = men, D = days, H = hours per day, W = work done)

### Worked example

*12 men build a wall in 10 days working 8 hours a day. How many days will 16 men take working 6 hours a day?*

- (12 × 10 × 8) / 1 = (16 × D × 6) / 1
- 960 = 96D → **D = 10 days**

## Partnership

Profit is shared in the ratio of **capital × time invested**.

### Worked example

*A invests ₹5,000 for 12 months, B invests ₹6,000 for 8 months. They earn ₹9,000 profit.  
Split it.*

- A's weight = 5000 × 12 = 60,000
- B's weight = 6000 × 8 = 48,000
- Ratio = 60 : 48 = **5 : 4**
- Total parts 9 → one part = 1,000 → **A gets ₹5,000, B gets ₹4,000**

**Working vs sleeping partner:** a working partner's salary or commission comes off the top  
*before* the remaining profit is divided by the capital ratio. Read carefully.

---

## Ages

Ages questions are ratio questions with a time shift. Set up with a multiplier and add the years to **both** people.

### Worked example

*The ratio of A's and B's ages is 4 : 3. In 6 years it will be 6 : 5. Find their present ages.*

- A = 4k, B = 3k
- (4k + 6) / (3k + 6) = 6/5
- 5(4k + 6) = 6(3k + 6) → 20k + 30 = 18k + 36 → **k = 3**
- **A is 12, B is 9.** Check: 18/15 = 6/5. ✓

## Practice set

#### Q1. Divide ₹2,400 among A, B and C in the ratio 3 : 5 : 8.

Total parts = 16 → one part = 150.  
**A = ₹450, B = ₹750, C = ₹1,200**

#### Q2. If A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 6 : 7, find A : B : C.

Make B match: multiply first by 6, second by 4 → A : B = 18 : 24, B : C = 24 : 28.  
**A : B : C = 18 : 24 : 28 = 9 : 12 : 14**

#### Q3. The ratio of two numbers is 5 : 7. If 6 is subtracted from each, the ratio becomes 2 : 3. Find the numbers.

Let them be 5k and 7k. (5k − 6)/(7k − 6) = 2/3  
15k − 18 = 14k − 12 → **k = 6** → numbers are **30 and 42**  
*Check: 24/36 = 2/3 ✓*

#### Q4. Two numbers are in the ratio 4 : 5. If their LCM is 180, find the numbers.

Numbers are 4k and 5k, which are co-prime multiples, so LCM = 20k.  
20k = 180 → k = 9 → **36 and 45**

#### Q5. ₹6,300 is divided among A, B and C such that A gets half of what B gets, and B gets half of what C gets. Find each share.

A = B/2, B = C/2 → let C = 4k, B = 2k, A = k. Ratio 1 : 2 : 4, total 7 parts.  
One part = 900 → **A = ₹900, B = ₹1,800, C = ₹3,600**

#### Q6. If 40% of A equals 60% of B, find A : B.

0.4A = 0.6B → A/B = 0.6/0.4 = 3/2  
**A : B = 3 : 2**

#### Q7. The mean proportional between 9 and 49?

√(9 × 49) = 3 × 7 = **21**

#### Q8. 15 men can build a wall in 20 days working 6 hours a day. In how many days will 18 men build it working 5 hours a day?

(15 × 20 × 6) = (18 × D × 5) → 1800 = 90D → **D = 20 days**

#### Q9. A, B and C invest ₹8,000, ₹10,000 and ₹12,000 for 6, 8 and 10 months respectively. Divide a profit of ₹9,300.

Weights: 8000×6 = 48,000 · 10000×8 = 80,000 · 12000×10 = 120,000  
Ratio = 48 : 80 : 120 = **6 : 10 : 15** → 31 parts → one part = 9300/31 = 300  
**A = ₹1,800, B = ₹3,000, C = ₹4,500**

> If the total does not divide cleanly by the number of parts, re-check your ratio before
> anything else — a wrong ratio is far more likely than a deliberately ugly number.

#### Q10. The present ages of A and B are in the ratio 5 : 6. Six years hence the ratio will be 6 : 7. Find their present ages.

(5k + 6)/(6k + 6) = 6/7 → 35k + 42 = 36k + 36 → **k = 6**  
**A = 30, B = 36** *(Check: 36/42 = 6/7 ✓)*

#### Q11. A sum of money is divided among A, B and C so that for every ₹3 A gets, B gets ₹4 and C gets ₹5. If C's share is ₹200 more than A's, find the total.

Ratio 3 : 4 : 5. C − A = 2 parts = 200 → one part = 100.  
Total = 12 parts = **₹1,200**

#### Q12. If x : y = 2 : 3, find (3x + 2y) : (2x + 5y).

Put x = 2, y = 3: (6 + 6) : (4 + 15) = **12 : 19**

> Substituting the simplest numbers that fit the ratio is almost always faster than algebra.
> Use it on every "find the value of this expression" ratio question.

---

## Traps

- "The ratio of A to B" is A/B. Reversing it flips your whole answer.
- When something is added, it is added to **both** terms — and to both *before* forming the
  new ratio.
- Ratios have no units. 3 : 4 could be 3 kg : 4 kg or 30 kg : 40 kg. Only the multiplier k
  carries the size.
- In partnership, unequal **time** is the thing people miss. Always compute capital × time.
- If a ratio "is increased by 20%", multiply that term by 6/5 — do not add 20 to it.
