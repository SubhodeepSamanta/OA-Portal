# How to use this

Round Zero is everything a company tests **before** anyone reads your code: quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal ability, and CS fundamentals.

It is not the hard part. It is the part that silently removes people. At a typical DIT drive, 1000+ students sit the online test and a few hundred clear it — and most of those who fail, fail on arithmetic and grammar they already know, under a clock they were not ready for.

---

## The single most important fact

**There is no negative marking** in the written tests of TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant,  
Capgemini and Accenture.

That has three consequences and you should behave as if they are laws:

1. **Never leave a question blank.** A blind guess is worth +0.25 expected marks. A blank
   is worth exactly zero. In the last 60 seconds of a section, fill every remaining bubble.
2. **Sections are separately timed and you cannot go back.** Wipro gives Quant 16 minutes
   for 16 questions. If you spend 4 minutes on question 3, you have already lost.
3. **Speed is a bigger lever than depth.** A student who can do 20 easy sums in 20 minutes
   beats a student who can do 8 hard sums perfectly.

> Target: **60 seconds per quant question, 30 seconds per verbal question.** If a question
> is not going to fall inside 60 seconds, mark a guess and move on. This one habit is worth
> more than any chapter in this shelf.

## What actually gets asked

Rough weightage across the big Indian recruiters, by share of the whole test:

| Block | Share | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Quantitative | ~30% | Every company |
| Reasoning + Verbal | ~38% | Every company |
| CS fundamentals + pseudocode | ~32% | Infosys, Accenture, Capgemini, Cognizant, all interviews |

Inside quant, the arithmetic family — percentages, ratio, averages, profit & loss, interest, time & work, time-speed-distance — is over half the section. Number system alone is the single biggest topic. Geometry, mensuration and P&C are much smaller.

This is why the sheets are tagged **must do / should do / if time**. The "must do" set is about 60% of the shelf and covers roughly 85% of the marks. Do it in order. Do not start with permutations because it looks interesting.

## The order to study in

Each topic builds on the one before it, so the sequence is not arbitrary.

**Week 1 — the arithmetic spine.** Percentages → Ratio & Proportion → Averages →  
Profit & Loss → Interest. Almost every other quant topic is one of these wearing a costume. Profit & loss *is* percentages. Alligation *is* weighted averages.

**Week 2 — rates and counting.** Time & Work → Time, Speed & Distance → Trains & Boats →  
Numbers/LCM-HCF → Data Interpretation. Time & Work and TSD are the same idea (rate × time  
= quantity) so learn them back to back.

**Week 3 — reasoning and verbal.** These are the highest marks-per-hour on the whole shelf. Reasoning is pure pattern recognition — you are not learning theory, you are learning to recognise a shape you have seen before. Verbal is six grammar rules that account for most error-spotting questions.

**Week 4 — CS fundamentals + pseudocode.** OOPS, OS, DBMS, CN. These pay twice: in the written test and again in the interview, where 40–50% of questions come from here.

Then loop back to the "should do" sheets with whatever time is left.

## A 30-day plan

Assume 2 hours a day. Adjust down, not out — it is better to do half of this properly than all of it badly.

| Days | Morning hour | Evening hour |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Simplification, Percentages, Ratio, Averages, Profit & Loss | Series, Coding–decoding, Blood relations, Directions |
| 6–10 | Interest, Time & Work, TSD, Trains & Boats, Numbers | Seating arrangement, Syllogism, Data sufficiency |
| 11–14 | DI, Progressions, Algebra, P&C, Probability | Puzzles, Grammar core, Error spotting |
| 15 | **Mock 3 — Wipro speed drill**, then review it | Sentence completion, Para jumbles |
| 16–20 | Geometry, Clocks & Calendars, revision of week 1 | RC, Vocabulary, Email writing |
| 21–24 | OOPS, OS, DBMS, CN | Pseudocode, DSA theory, MS Office |
| 25 | **Mock 1 — TCS NQT**, then review it | HR interview sheet: write your four STAR stories |
| 26–28 | Re-read every topic you got wrong in Mock 1 | Group discussion, Communication assessment |
| 29 | **Mock 2 — Infosys**, then review it | Rehearse "tell me about yourself" out loud |
| 30 | Re-sit Mock 3 and compare your pace to day 15 | Review every unticked checkpoint |

**The mock days matter most.** Sit each one with a timer, no pausing, nothing looked up — then
spend as long reviewing the mistakes as you spent taking the test. A topic you got wrong twice
is worth ten topics you have never seen.

Note that **Mock 3 appears twice**, on day 15 and day 30. That is deliberate: it measures pace
rather than knowledge, so re-sitting it tells you whether the month actually made you faster.

## How the checkpoints work

Every sheet ends with a list of checkpoints — tick one only when you could do that thing  
**cold, in an exam, without notes**. They save to your account, and the shelf shows how far along you are.

The honest use of a checkbox is as a filter for revision: on day 28 you want to look only at the boxes that are still empty. Ticking a box you have not earned removes the only thing that makes this useful.

## Exam-day tactics

- **Do a first pass picking off the easy ones.** Do not answer in order. Sweep the section,
  solve everything that takes under 45 seconds, then come back for the rest if the platform allows it.
- **Options are data.** Approximate first. If the options are 12%, 34%, 67%, 91%, you rarely
  need the exact value — a rough estimate kills three of them.
- **Back-substitute.** For messy algebra, plugging the four options into the question is
  usually faster than solving it.
- **Round aggressively in DI.** DI options are spread far apart on purpose. 4,978/23 is 216,
  and nobody needs more precision than that.
- **In verbal, trust your ear once.** Your first instinct on grammar is right more often
  than your second. Re-reading a sentence five times manufactures doubt.
- **Keep a running guess-list.** If you skip Q7 and Q12, note them. With one minute left,
  fill them in rather than staring at Q20.

- [ ] I have accepted the 60-second-per-question rule and will guess-and-move rather than fight
- [ ] I know the study order: arithmetic spine → rates & counting → reasoning/verbal → CS fundamentals
- [ ] I have picked which days of the 30-day plan I am actually starting on
