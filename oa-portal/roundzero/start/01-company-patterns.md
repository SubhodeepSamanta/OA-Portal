# Exact test pattern, company by company

DIT University's Career Services Cell brings 400+ recruiters. The ones that hire in volume from CSE/IT are the IT services majors — **TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Capgemini,  
Accenture** — alongside product and consulting firms (Amazon, Adobe, Oracle, Dell, Deloitte,  
Goldman Sachs, IBM, Lenovo).

The volume recruiters are where most offers come from, and they are the ones with a fixed, knowable written test. Learn their patterns; they change slowly.

> Patterns drift year to year and drive to drive. Treat the numbers below as the shape of
> the test, not a contract — always read the official mail your placement cell sends.

---

## TCS — National Qualifier Test (NQT)

The hardest of the volume recruiters, and the one worth preparing hardest for.

**Foundation section — 65 questions, 76 minutes, sectionally timed**

| Section | Qs | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical Ability | 20 | 25 min |
| Verbal Ability | 25 | 26 min |
| Reasoning Ability | 20 | 25 min |

**Advanced section** (only for TCS Digital / TCS Prime profiles): Advanced Quant +  
Advanced Reasoning (~14–16 questions, 25 min shared) and Advanced Coding (2 questions,  
90 minutes).

- **No negative marking.**
- Numerical: number systems, percentages, profit/loss, time-speed-distance, ratios, ages,
  averages, permutations, probability, geometry, data interpretation.
- Verbal has moved toward applied English: **sentence completion in bulk, passage recall,
  and one professional email-writing task.** Do not skip the email sheet — it is free marks and most people wing it.
- Reasoning: series, seating arrangement, blood relations, coding–decoding, syllogism, data
  sufficiency, visual reasoning.

**What to prioritise:** TCS sets genuinely harder quant than the others. If you can clear  
TCS's numerical section, every other company's is comfortable.

## Infosys

**~5 sections, roughly 110–120 minutes total.**

| Section | Time |
|---|---|
| Mathematical Ability | ~35 min |
| Logical Reasoning | ~25 min |
| Verbal Ability | ~20 min |
| Pseudocode | ~30 min |
| Puzzle Solving | shared |

- Maths: number series, time-speed-distance, time & work, P&C, probability, profit & loss,
  ratios, averages, simplification, DI.
- Reasoning: syllogisms, coding–decoding, blood relations, data sufficiency, direction
  sense, statement & conclusion, seating arrangement.
- **Pseudocode is a full section here.** Loops, operators, conditionals, arrays, functions,
  recursion, basic data structures — read the pseudocode sheet properly.
- Puzzles: Sudoku-style grids, missing numbers, pattern puzzles.
- A score around **65–70% is generally considered safe**; the actual cutoff moves with the
  drive and the college.

## Wipro — Elite NTH

**Tight per-section timing — roughly one minute per question, and that is the whole game.**

| Section | Qs | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Quantitative Aptitude | 16 | 16 min |
| Logical Reasoning | 14 | 14 min |
| Verbal Ability | 18 | 18 min |
| Written Communication (essay) | 1 | 20 min |

- **No negative marking.**
- The essay is a real scored component. It is graded on structure, grammar and length far
  more than on ideas — see the email & essay sheet.
- With 60 seconds per question there is no time to derive anything. This is the company
  where memorised shortcuts (fraction-to-percent table, squares to 30, work-rate LCM trick) directly convert into marks.

## Cognizant — GenC / GenC Pro

Delivered on the AMCAT platform.

**Aptitude round: 25 questions in 35 minutes**

| Section | Qs |
|---|---|
| Quantitative Aptitude | 10 |
| Logical Reasoning | 10 |
| Verbal Ability | 5 |

Followed by a technical round (~105–120 minutes depending on the cluster you pick) and a communication assessment. Across all rounds it runs to roughly 143 questions/tasks.

- **No negative marking.**
- The aptitude round is short and comparatively gentle — the technical cluster is where
  candidates are actually separated. Know your OOPS, DBMS and one language properly.

## Capgemini

The odd one out: **no conventional quant section at all.**

| Section | Qs | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Pseudocode (MCQ) | 25 | 25 min |
| English Communication | 30 | 30 min |
| Game-Based Aptitude | 4 games | ~27 min |
| Behavioural / PowerSkills | — | ~20–25 min |
| Coding round | — | +45 min |

- The **game-based cognitive round** draws 4 games at random from a pool of ~24, each about
  6 minutes. They test working memory, attention, pattern-spotting and risk calibration.  
  You cannot study them; you can practise similar games so the interface does not surprise you, and you can be well-rested.
- **Pseudocode and English are the two you can actually prepare** — and they are 55 of the
  scored questions. Weight your preparation accordingly.
- **No negative marking.** Total sitting is around three hours.

## Accenture

**Cognitive & Technical Assessment: 92 questions in 90 minutes**, then coding (2 questions,  
45 min) and a communication assessment (20 questions, 20 min).

Cognitive:

| Block | Qs | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal Ability | 17 | synonyms/antonyms, error detection, RC, para jumbles, sentence completion & improvement |
| Reasoning Ability | 18 | data arrangements (7–8), flowcharts, syllogisms, statement–conclusion, argument & assumption, data sufficiency |
| Numerical Ability | 15 | number & letter series, odd man out, analogies, coding–decoding, visual reasoning |

Technical:

| Block | Qs | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| MS Office & applications | 12 | Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, shortcuts, browser basics, command prompt |
| Pseudocode | 18 | loops, arrays, recursion, functions, conditionals, DS basics |
| Networking, Security & Cloud | 10 | firewalls, encryption, cloud models, client–server |

- Note that Accenture files series/analogy/coding–decoding under **"Numerical Ability"** —
  it is reasoning by content, so do not panic when the section header says numerical.
- The **MS Office block is unique to Accenture** and is pure recall. An hour spent on Excel
  formula names and Word/PowerPoint shortcuts is one of the highest-return hours available.

## The interview round (all of them)

Once you clear the written test, **40–50% of technical interview questions come from CS fundamentals** — OS, DBMS, CN, OOPS — not from DSA.

- **TCS** leans on DBMS (SQL queries, normalization) and OOPS (four pillars, polymorphism),
  with basic OS and occasional CN.
- **Infosys** expects all four plus a language you nominate.
- **Cognizant/Capgemini/Accenture** stay close to definitions and small SQL.

Every one of them asks some version of: *four pillars of OOPS*, *process vs thread*,  
*deadlock and its four conditions*, *normalization up to 3NF*, *ACID*, *DELETE vs TRUNCATE vs DROP*, *TCP vs UDP*, *OSI layers*, *what happens when you type google.com*. Those nine are close to compulsory. They are all in the CS fundamentals track.

## Reading the field

| If you are targeting | Spend your time on |
|---|---|
| TCS | Hard quant + verbal + the email task |
| Infosys | Pseudocode + puzzles + maths |
| Wipro | Speed drills + the essay |
| Cognizant | The technical cluster, not the aptitude |
| Capgemini | Pseudocode + English (there is no quant) |
| Accenture | MS Office + pseudocode + networking basics |
| Product firms (Amazon, Adobe, Oracle) | DSA in the Question Bank; aptitude barely features |
