# Vocabulary, idioms, one-word substitution

Accenture asks 3–4 synonym/antonym questions per paper, and vocabulary underpins sentence completion and RC too. Tagged **should do** because it rewards steady exposure rather than a single study session — start early and do a little daily.

## How to actually study this

Rote-learning 500 words does not work and you will forget them by the drive. What works:

1. **Learn roots, not words.** One root unlocks fifteen words.
2. **Learn words in a sentence**, never as a bare pair.
3. **Do 10 minutes daily**, not 3 hours on a Sunday.
4. **Prioritise the words that actually recur** — the lists below are drawn from what appears
   in these papers.

## Exam technique when you do not know the word

1. **Break it into root and prefix.** *Malevolent* → mal (bad) + vol (wish) → wishing harm.
2. **Check the prefix for polarity.** *a-*, *anti-*, *mis-*, *ob-*, *dis-*, *in-*, *un-* all
   point negative; *eu-*, *bene-*, *pro-* point positive. Even guessing the *direction* halves your options.
3. **In synonym questions**, eliminate options whose polarity is clearly opposite.
4. **Guess.** There is no negative marking.

### Decoding an unknown word

#### Q39. What is the likely meaning of MALEDICTION?

*mal* = bad, *dict* = say → **a curse**

#### Q40. What is the likely meaning of PERSPICACIOUS?

*per* = through, *spic* = see → seeing through things → **having keen insight**

#### Q41. What is the likely meaning of MAGNANIMOUS?

*magn* = great, *anim* = mind/spirit → **generous, big-hearted**

## Traps

- Synonym questions ask for the **closest** meaning, not an exact one.
- Antonym questions: read the instruction. Candidates lose marks by picking a synonym in an
  antonym question — it is always among the options.
- Idioms are **not literal**. "Take with a pinch of salt" has nothing to do with salt.
- In one-word substitution, several options are often near-misses; the one that matches
  *precisely* wins. *Agnostic* is not *atheist*.
- Do not confuse credible/credulous, eminent/imminent, principal/principle.
