# The grammar rules that get tested

Error spotting, sentence correction and sentence improvement are all the same skill, and they test the **same handful of rules** year after year. Learn these eight and you cover most of what appears.

---

## Rule 1 — Subject–verb agreement

The verb agrees with the **subject**, not with the nearest noun.

> ✗ The **quality** of the mangoes **were** poor.
> ✓ The **quality** of the mangoes **was** poor.

The subject is *quality* (singular). "of the mangoes" is a prepositional phrase and cannot contain the subject. **Mentally delete every phrase beginning with a preposition**, then check the verb.

**The specific cases that get tested:**

| Structure | Verb agrees with |
|---|---|
| A, **along with / as well as / together with / besides** B | **A only** |
| A **and** B | plural |
| **Either** A **or** B / **Neither** A **nor** B | the **nearer** subject |
| **Each / every / either / neither / one of** | **singular** |
| **Number of** X | plural &nbsp; · &nbsp; **The number of** X → singular |
| Collective nouns (team, jury, government) | singular in Indian/British usage |
| Uncountables: news, mathematics, physics, politics | **singular** |
| Always plural: scissors, trousers, spectacles, goods | **plural** |

> ✓ The manager, along with his staff, **is** attending.
> ✓ Neither the students nor the teacher **was** present. *(nearer = teacher)*
> ✓ Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. *(nearer = students)*
> ✓ **One of** the boys **is** absent.
> ✓ **Each of** the answers **is** correct.

## Rule 2 — Tense consistency

Do not switch tense without a reason.

> ✗ He **came** in and **sits** down.
> ✓ He **came** in and **sat** down.

**Reported speech** shifts one step back:

> He said, "I am tired." → He said that he **was** tired.

**Exception:** universal truths stay in the present.
> The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun. ✓

**Time markers that fix the tense:**

| Marker | Tense |
|---|---|
| since / for + a period | present perfect or perfect continuous |
| ago | simple past |
| by the time / before + past event | past perfect for the earlier action |
| yesterday, last week | simple past |

> ✓ I **have been living** here **since** 2019.
> ✓ He **had left** before I **arrived**. *(the earlier action takes past perfect)*

---

## Rule 3 — Articles

- **a** before a consonant *sound*, **an** before a vowel *sound* — the sound, not the letter
  - **a** university, **a** European, **a** one-rupee note *(these begin with y- and w- sounds)*
  - **an** hour, **an** honest man, **an** MBA *(silent h; "em-bee-ay" starts with a vowel sound)*
- **the** for something already specified, unique, or superlative
  - the sun, the Ganges, the best, the first
- **No article** before abstract nouns, meals, languages, or plurals in general
  - ✓ Honesty is the best policy. · ✓ He speaks English. · ✓ Dogs are loyal.

---

## Rule 4 — Prepositions

The commonest single source of errors, and mostly a matter of fixed pairs. The high-frequency ones:

| Verb/adjective | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| depend / rely | **on** | depends on you |
| consist | **of** | consists of five parts |
| comprise | **(nothing)** | the team **comprises** five members |
| discuss | **(nothing)** | discuss the matter |
| married | **to** | married to her |
| superior / inferior / senior / junior | **to** | senior to me |
| different | **from** | different from mine |
| angry | **with** a person, **at** a thing | angry with him, angry at the delay |
| accuse | **of** | accused of theft |
| capable | **of** | capable of winning |
| suffer | **from** | suffering from fever |
| die | **of** a disease, **from** a cause | died of cancer |
| congratulate | **on** | congratulated him on his success |
| prefer X | **to** Y | prefers tea to coffee |

> ✗ He is senior **than** me. ✓ He is senior **to** me.
> ✗ Discuss **about** the matter. ✓ **Discuss** the matter.
> ✗ Comprises **of** five members. ✓ **Comprises** five members.

Those last two — *discuss about* and *comprises of* — are near-guaranteed to appear.

## Rule 5 — Pronouns

**Agreement:** a pronoun matches its antecedent in number.
> ✗ Every student must bring **their** book. → ✓ **his or her** book *(formal/exam usage)*

**Case:** use the subject form after "than" and "as" when a verb is implied.
> ✓ He is taller than **I** (am). ✓ She sings as well as **he** (does).

**Order of persons:** 231 for good news, 123 when confessing a fault.
> ✓ **You**, **he** and **I** will go. *(2nd, 3rd, 1st)*
> ✓ **I**, **you** and **he** are to blame. *(1st, 2nd, 3rd)*

**Reflexives** need an antecedent in the same sentence.
> ✗ **Myself** Rahul. ✓ **I am** Rahul.

---

## Rule 6 — Modifiers

A modifying phrase attaches to whatever noun follows it. Put it next to the right one.

> ✗ **Walking down the road**, the trees looked beautiful. *(the trees were walking)*
> ✓ **Walking down the road**, I found the trees beautiful.

**Only** is the most-misplaced word in English. Put it immediately before what it limits.
> "He **only** eats vegetables" (does nothing else with them) ≠ "He eats **only** vegetables".

---

## Rule 7 — Parallelism

Items in a list must share a grammatical form.

> ✗ She likes **reading**, **to swim** and **cycling**.
> ✓ She likes **reading**, **swimming** and **cycling**.

This applies to correlative pairs too — whatever follows *not only* must match whatever follows *but also*:

> ✗ He is not only good at maths **but also** he plays well.
> ✓ He is good not only **at maths** but also **at sport**.

---

## Rule 8 — Comparatives and degree

- Two things → **comparative** (bigger); three or more → **superlative** (biggest)
- No double comparatives: ✗ more better, ✗ most easiest
- **Comparisons must be between like things:**
  > ✗ The climate of Delhi is hotter than **Mumbai**. *(a climate vs a city)*
  > ✓ The climate of Delhi is hotter than **that of Mumbai**.

That last error appears in almost every error-spotting set.

## Practice set

Pick the correct option, then read the reason.

**Q1. The quality of the apples ______ not good.**  (is / are)  
Subject is *quality* (singular); "of the apples" is a prepositional phrase.  
**is**

**Q2. Neither the students nor the teacher ______ aware of the change.**  (was / were)  
Neither/nor agrees with the **nearer** subject — *teacher*, singular.  
**was**

**Q3. Neither the teacher nor the students ______ aware of the change.**  (was / were)  
Nearer subject is now *students*, plural.  
**were**

**Q4. The manager, along with his colleagues, ______ invited.**  (was / were)  
"Along with" does not make a singular subject plural.  
**was**

**Q5. One of the boys ______ broken the window.**  (has / have)  
"One of the" takes a singular verb.  
**has**

**Q6. The number of applicants ______ increased.**  (has / have)  
"**The** number of" is singular; "**a** number of" is plural.  
**has**

**Q7. Mathematics ______ my favourite subject.**  (is / are)  
Subjects ending in -ics are singular.  
**is**

**Q8. He is senior ______ me by two years.**  (than / to)  
Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior take **to**.  
**to**

**Q9. The committee ______ discussing the proposal.**  (is / are)  
Collective noun acting as one body → singular in Indian and British exam usage.  
**is**

**Q10. She has been working here ______ 2018.**  (since / for)  
*Since* takes a point in time; *for* takes a duration.  
**since**

**Q11. He said that the sun ______ in the east.**  (rose / rises)  
Universal truths stay in the present even in reported speech.  
**rises**

**Q12. Each of the answers ______ correct.**  (is / are)  
"Each of" is singular.  
**is**

**Q13. It is I who ______ responsible.**  (am / is)  
The relative pronoun *who* takes the person and number of its antecedent, *I*.  
**am**

**Q14. He is taller than ______.**  (I / me)  
A verb is implied: "than I am".  
**I**

**Q15. ______ university in Dehradun.**  (A / An)  
*University* begins with a "yoo" consonant sound.  
**A**

**Q16. He was ______ honest man.**  (a / an)  
*Honest* begins with a silent h, so a vowel sound.  
**an**

**Q17. The team ______ comprises eleven players.**  (comprises / comprises of)  
*Comprise* takes no preposition.  
**comprises**

**Q18. Let us discuss ______ the matter.**  (about / — )  
*Discuss* takes no preposition.  
**— (no preposition)**

**Q19. The climate of Mumbai is more humid than ______ Delhi.**  (— / that of)  
Compare like with like: a climate with a climate.  
**that of**

**Q20. Scarcely had he entered the room ______ the lights went out.**  (than / when)  
*Scarcely/hardly … when*; *no sooner … than*.  
**when**

**Q21. He would rather ______ than argue.**  (to leave / leave)  
*Would rather* takes a bare infinitive.  
**leave**

**Q22. She likes reading, swimming and ______.**  (to cycle / cycling)  
Parallelism.  
**cycling**

#### Q23. Identify the error: "Walking along the beach, the sunset looked beautiful."

Dangling modifier — the sunset was not walking.

**Correct: "Walking along the beach, I found the sunset beautiful."**

#### Q24. Identify the error: "He only drinks coffee in the morning."

Misplaced *only* — as written it means drinking is the only thing he does.

**Correct: "He drinks only coffee in the morning."**

**Q25. Order the persons: "___, ___ and ___ are to blame."** (I / you / he)  
Confessing a fault takes 1-2-3 order.  
**I, you and he**

## Traps

- The verb agrees with the subject, never with the noun immediately before it.
- *One of the* + plural noun + **singular** verb.
- *Comprises of* and *discuss about* are always wrong.
- *Senior/junior/superior/inferior* take **to**, never *than*.
- Compare like with like — add *that of* or *those of*.
- Universal truths stay present tense even in reported speech.

---

## Checkpoints

- [ ] I delete prepositional phrases before checking subject–verb agreement
- [ ] I know "along with / as well as" does not make a singular subject plural
- [ ] I know either/or and neither/nor agree with the nearer subject
- [ ] I know "one of the" and "each of" take a singular verb
- [ ] I use a/an by sound, not by letter (a university, an hour, an MBA)
- [ ] I know the fixed prepositions, especially senior **to**, different **from**, comprises (no of), discuss (no about)
- [ ] I use the subject pronoun after "than" and "as"
- [ ] I know the 231 / 123 order of persons
- [ ] I can spot a dangling modifier and a misplaced "only"
- [ ] I keep lists parallel, including after not only / but also
- [ ] I compare like with like using "that of" / "those of"
